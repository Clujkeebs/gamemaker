# The name

## Romp

> **romp** *(v.)* — to play in a lively, boisterous way.
> **romp** *(n.)* — a spell of that; also, a book, film or game that is
> lighthearted and fun. "A delightful romp."

That second sense is the reason. *Romp* is the word critics already reach for
when something is short, silly and enjoyable — which is exactly what comes out
of this. You type a half-formed idea, a game falls out, you poke at it until
it's fun, and you throw the link at your friends.

**Domain: `romps.ai`.** Published games live at `<slug>.romps.ai`.

### Why this one, specifically

**It's a word, not a construction.** Machine-generated product names are built
by gluing morphemes: `-forge`, `-nexus`, `-flux`, `-mint`, `-loom`, `-spark`,
`-genesis`, `-craft`, `-ify`, `-ly`, `-labs`, `-AI`. *GameForge* is a textbook
example — a category noun bolted to a maker-verb. Romp is a single dictionary
word nobody assembled.

**It names the output, not the machine.** A generated thing is "a romp." That
gives the product a unit — you made a romp, here's my romp, look at this romp —
which a name like *GameForge* never provides.

**It survives the subdomain test.** The name's most-seen placement isn't the
homepage, it's the tail of every published game's URL:

```
catninja.romps.ai
dogsled-panic.romps.ai
my-terrible-first-game.romps.ai
```

Those look like games. `catninja.gameforge.app` looks like output.

**One syllable, no spelling question.** Four letters, one vowel sound, nothing
silent or ambiguous. It survives being said out loud in a demo without a
follow-up spelling — which two-syllable *Rumpus*, its predecessor here, didn't
quite manage.

**It has no built-in ceiling.** "Game" isn't in the name, so 2D-in-the-browser
isn't a promise the name makes.

### On the plural domain

The brand is **Romp**; the domain is **romps.ai**. `romp.ai` was already taken,
as was every other single dictionary word in this family — `antics`, `hijinks`,
`ruckus`, `caper`, `frolic`, `rowdy`, `scamp`, `larks`, `tumble`, `shindig`.
That is the normal state of `.ai` in 2026.

The plural is not a consolation prize here. It reads as *the collection* — the
place all the romps live — and each published game is one of them. "Make a
romp, it lands on romps.ai" is coherent in a way that a hyphen or a `get-`
prefix would not be.

### Voice that falls out of it

Loud, cheap, fast, unprecious. Buttons say **Make a romp**, not "Generate."
Publishing says **Let it loose.** Failure says **That one broke. Try again.**
Never apologize for a weird game — a weird game is the whole point.

### Usage

- Always capitalized: **Romp**. Never ROMP.
- Root domain: `romps.ai`. Published games live at `<slug>.romps.ai`.
- The generation engine is *Romp*, not "the Romp AI." The `.ai` is an address,
  not part of the name — don't write "Romp.ai" in prose.
- A generated game is **a romp**, lowercase.
- Product nouns, all lowercase in prose: a **game** (what you made), a
  **template** (the engine archetype under it), a **config** (the JSON the
  model writes), the **arcade page** (the landing page it publishes with).

### Runners-up

Kept so the choice is legible, not to be revisited. All were taken on `.ai`:

- **Ruckus** — same energy, but a ruckus is a disturbance; Romp is fun.
- **Antics** — lovely, plural-only, harder to make a verb of.
- **Hijinks** — great meaning, awkward to spell out loud.
- **Caper** — implies a heist specifically, which narrows it.
