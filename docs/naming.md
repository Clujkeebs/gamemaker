# The name

## Rumpus

> **rumpus** *(n.)* — a noisy, boisterous commotion. In American English since
> the 1760s; a *rumpus room* is the room in the house where the kids are allowed
> to make a mess.

That's the product. You type a half-formed idea, a game falls out, you poke at
it until it's fun, and you throw the link at your friends. It's a mess, on
purpose, and that's the good part.

### Why this one, specifically

**It's a word, not a construction.** Machine-generated product names are built by
gluing morphemes: `-forge`, `-nexus`, `-flux`, `-mint`, `-loom`, `-spark`,
`-genesis`, `-craft`, `-ify`, `-ly`, `-labs`, `-AI`. *GameForge* is a textbook
example — a category noun bolted to a maker-verb. Rumpus is a single dictionary
word with two centuries of use that nobody assembled.

**It reads as a place, not a tool.** Rumpus room, rec room, arcade. The product
is somewhere you go to mess around, not a utility you operate. That framing is
also the pitch: the thing you make here is meant to be shared and played, not
"generated" and archived.

**It survives the subdomain test.** The name's most-seen placement isn't the
homepage — it's the tail of every published game's URL:

```
catninja.rumpus.gg
dogsled-panic.rumpus.gg
my-terrible-first-game.rumpus.gg
```

Those look like games. `catninja.gameforge.app` looks like output.

**It's sayable and spellable.** Two syllables, stress on the first, no vowel
puzzles, no silent letters, no ambiguity between the spoken and typed form. It
survives being said out loud in a demo without a follow-up spelling.

**It has no built-in ceiling.** "Game" isn't in the name, so 2D-in-the-browser
isn't a promise the name makes. If this ever grows into 3D, multiplayer,
interactive toys, or anything else you'd make in a rumpus room, the name still
fits.

### Voice that falls out of it

Loud, cheap, fast, unprecious. Buttons say **Make a rumpus**, not "Generate."
Publishing says **Let it loose.** Failure says **That one broke. Try again.**
Never apologize for a weird game — a weird game is the whole point.

### Usage

- Always capitalized: **Rumpus**. Never RUMPUS, never rumpus.ai.
- Root domain: `rumpus.gg`. Published games live at `<slug>.rumpus.gg`.
- The generation engine is *Rumpus*, not "the Rumpus AI." Don't put "AI" in the
  name anywhere it can be avoided; it's a description of the plumbing, not the
  product.
- Product nouns, all lowercase in prose: a **game** (what you made), a
  **template** (the engine archetype under it), a **config** (the JSON the AI
  writes), the **arcade page** (the landing page it publishes with).

### Runners-up

Kept here so the choice is legible, not to be revisited:

- **Clatter** — same energy, but crowded in dev tooling and slightly harsher.
- **Bunkhouse** — great texture, wrong connotation (sleeping, not playing).
- **Coin-op** — perfect for arcade, but hyphenated names are a permanent tax.
- **Skitter** — charming, but reads as movement, not play, and is near-homophonous
  with several existing products.
